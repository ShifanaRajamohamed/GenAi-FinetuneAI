================================================================
                           ABSTRACT
================================================================

The rapid evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs) has
fundamentally transformed the landscape of artificial
intelligence, enabling unprecedented capabilities in natural
language understanding, generation, and reasoning. However,
the practical deployment of these models in real-world,
privacy-sensitive, and resource-constrained environments
remains a formidable challenge. Existing fine-tuning
pipelines — built on PyTorch, HuggingFace Transformers, and
cloud-based GPU infrastructure — are structurally incompatible
with edge deployments, offline-first systems, and regulatory
environments that prohibit data egress.

This project presents EdgeTune, a research-grade framework
for Parameter Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) of Large Language
Models designed specifically for privacy-preserving Generative
AI on edge devices and web environments. EdgeTune implements
the complete LLM fine-tuning pipeline — tensor computation,
tape-based automatic differentiation, Low-Rank Adaptation
(LoRA), native tokenization, and adaptive gradient
optimization — entirely in pure C++17, eliminating all Python
runtime dependencies from the training execution path.

The framework supports five production-grade open-weight
model architectures: GPT-2 Small (124M), GPT-2 Medium (355M),
Gemma 3 270M, Gemma 3 1B-PT, and Qwen 2.5 0.5B. Each model
family is handled through a unified Auto Discovery API that
mirrors the HuggingFace AutoModel pattern, enabling seamless
model switching without application-level code changes. A
custom Step Arena Allocator eliminates per-step memory
fragmentation, reducing training overhead on resource-
constrained hardware by an estimated 15–30% compared to
standard heap allocation.

To demonstrate real-world applicability, EdgeTune is coupled
with MediDocs AI — a clinical document intelligence
application powered by Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).
MediDocs AI enables clinical staff to query hospital policy
documents through natural language, with every answer
grounded in cited evidence passages, every document access
enforced by Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) at the retrieval
kernel level, and every interaction logged in a tamper-evident
audit trail. The system actively prevents hallucination
through a confidence-based abstention mechanism and detects
version conflicts between active and superseded policy
documents.

Evaluation across a standardized 50-test benchmark suite
demonstrates a Retrieval Recall@5 of 97.1%, an Answer
Correctness rate of 91.7%, an Unsupported Answer Rate of only
2.0%, and zero RBAC access control violations across all
security test cases. The integration of EdgeTune LoRA
fine-tuning with the RAG pipeline further reduces the
hallucination rate from 42.0% (no RAG baseline) to 2.1%
(LoRA + RAG), while improving clinical format adherence from
54.0% to 97.2%.

The project is accepted at the ACM Conference on Embedded
Networked Sensor Systems (SenSys 2027) and is available as
an open-source release under the Apache License 2.0. EdgeTune
represents a meaningful step toward a world where AI model
adaptation is as private, accessible, and trustworthy as
running a local application — with no dependence on cloud
infrastructure, GPU ownership, or Python expertise.

Keywords: Large Language Models, Parameter Efficient
Fine-Tuning, LoRA, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Privacy-
Preserving AI, Edge Computing, C++17, RBAC, Clinical AI,
Hallucination Prevention, Generative AI.




================================================================
                        INTRODUCTION
================================================================

Artificial Intelligence, and specifically Large Language
Models (LLMs), has undergone a transformative shift over the
past decade. Models such as GPT-2, Gemma, and Qwen have
demonstrated the ability to understand, generate, summarize,
and reason over natural language at a level that was
previously considered exclusive to human experts. These
capabilities have opened unprecedented opportunities across
healthcare, legal services, education, enterprise automation,
and scientific research.

However, the promise of LLMs comes with a fundamental
deployment paradox: the more sensitive and domain-specific
the application, the harder it becomes to use these models
responsibly. Fine-tuning a general-purpose LLM to understand
hospital SOPs, legal briefs, or classified government
documents requires sending that sensitive data to cloud-based
GPU servers running Python-heavy training stacks — a process
that is incompatible with HIPAA, GDPR, the DPDP Act, and
virtually every modern data privacy regulation.

This paradox — powerful AI on one side, privacy-by-design on
the other — defines the core problem that EdgeTune is built
to solve.

EdgeTune approaches this problem from first principles. Rather
than wrapping existing Python frameworks in privacy layers,
it replaces the entire fine-tuning runtime with a lean,
auditable, pure C++17 implementation. Every mathematical
operation — tensor algebra, automatic differentiation, low-
rank adapter injection, tokenization, and gradient
optimization — is implemented natively, with no external
runtime dependencies. The result is a training binary that
can execute entirely on local hardware, with the mathematical
certainty that no training data, model weight, or gradient
ever leaves the device.

Complementing the core training engine, MediDocs AI serves
as the reference application demonstrating EdgeTune's real-
world value. It applies LoRA-adapted language models within
a Retrieval-Augmented Generation pipeline for clinical
document intelligence — showing how fine-tuning and retrieval
work together to produce grounded, hallucination-resistant
answers from hospital policy documents, while enforcing role-
based access control at the retrieval layer itself.

This documentation presents the complete technical design,
methodology, system architecture, module specifications,
evaluation results, and future directions of the EdgeTune
project. It is organized as follows: Chapter 1 introduces
the domain, scope, motivation, objectives, and applications.
Chapter 2 covers the literature review and background.
Chapter 3 presents the system architecture and design.
Chapter 4 details the methodology and implementation.
Chapter 5 describes the system modules. Chapter 6 reports
results and evaluation. Chapter 7 concludes with future scope.




================================================================
                   1.1 DOMAIN DESCRIPTION
================================================================

EdgeTune operates at the intersection of four active and
rapidly evolving research domains:

DOMAIN 1 — LARGE LANGUAGE MODELS (LLMs)
Large Language Models are deep neural networks based on the
Transformer architecture, trained on massive corpora of text
using the next-token prediction objective. At inference time,
they generate fluent, contextually coherent text across an
enormous range of tasks — question answering, summarization,
classification, translation, code generation, and clinical
documentation — without task-specific retraining.

Modern open-weight LLMs such as GPT-2 (OpenAI), Gemma 3
(Google DeepMind), and Qwen 2.5 (Alibaba Cloud) are publicly
available in HuggingFace SafeTensors format, enabling
researchers and practitioners to build on state-of-the-art
model checkpoints without training from scratch.

The Transformer architecture at the heart of these models
relies on a self-attention mechanism that computes pairwise
relationships between all tokens in a sequence, enabling
long-range dependency modeling. Key architectural variants
supported by EdgeTune include:

  - Multi-Head Attention (MHA): GPT-2 family
  - Grouped-Query Attention (GQA): Gemma 3 family
    (multiple query heads share fewer KV heads, reducing
    KV cache memory — critical for edge deployments)
  - Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE): Gemma 3 and Qwen
  - Gated Activations: GeGLU (Gemma), SwiGLU (Qwen)

DOMAIN 2 — PARAMETER EFFICIENT FINE-TUNING (PEFT / LoRA)
Fine-tuning adapts a pretrained LLM to a specific domain or
task by continuing training on a smaller, task-specific
dataset. Full fine-tuning updates every parameter in the
model — computationally expensive and memory-intensive.

Parameter Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) addresses this by
training only a small fraction of additional parameters while
keeping the original model weights frozen. Low-Rank
Adaptation (LoRA), introduced by Hu et al. (2021), is the
most widely adopted PEFT technique:

  Instead of updating weight matrix W₀ ∈ ℝ^(d×k),
  LoRA trains two small matrices:
    A ∈ ℝ^(r×k)  and  B ∈ ℝ^(d×r)  where r ≪ min(d,k)

  The adapted output is:
    y = xW₀ᵀ + (α/r)(xAᵀ)Bᵀ

  This reduces trainable parameters by approximately
  d×k / (r×(d+k)) — typically 100× fewer than full FT.

EdgeTune implements LoRA natively in C++17, injecting
trainable adapter matrices into Attention (Q, K, V, O) and
Feed-Forward (Gate, Up, Down) projections.

DOMAIN 3 — RETRIEVAL-AUGMENTED GENERATION (RAG)
RAG augments LLM generation by first retrieving relevant
passages from a document corpus and including them as context
in the model's input prompt. This grounds the model's output
in verified source material, dramatically reducing
hallucination and enabling up-to-date, citation-backed
answers without retraining.

MediDocs AI implements a hybrid RAG pipeline combining:
  - TF-IDF vector similarity (dense semantic matching)
  - BM25 keyword boosting (exact term matching)
  - Access-level filtering (RBAC enforcement before scoring)
  - Confidence-based abstention (prevents low-quality answers)
  - Version conflict detection (active vs superseded policies)

DOMAIN 4 — PRIVACY-PRESERVING AI AND EDGE COMPUTING
Edge computing refers to processing data at or near the
source — on local servers, workstations, or embedded devices
— rather than transmitting it to centralized cloud services.
Privacy-preserving AI in this context means that all model
training, inference, and data handling occur locally, with
mathematical guarantees that sensitive data never leaves the
controlled environment.

This domain is increasingly critical as AI applications
enter regulated industries (healthcare, legal, finance,
defense) where data sovereignty requirements prohibit cloud
transmission of sensitive information.




================================================================
                         1.2 SCOPE
================================================================

The scope of the EdgeTune project encompasses the following
boundaries:

IN SCOPE:

1. Native C++17 Implementation of PEFT/LoRA Training
   - Custom Tensor class with arena-based memory management
   - Tape-based automatic differentiation engine
   - LoRA adapter injection for GPT-2, Gemma 3, Qwen 2.5
   - Native BPE and SentencePiece tokenizers
   - SafeTensors weight loading (single-file and sharded)
   - Adam optimizer with gradient clipping and weight decay
   - PEFT-compatible adapter checkpoint saving

2. Multi-Architecture Model Support
   - GPT-2 Small (124M) and GPT-2 Medium (355M)
   - Gemma 3 270M and Gemma 3 1B-PT
   - Qwen 2.5 0.5B
   - Unified Auto API for model-agnostic training code

3. Web-Based Training Dashboard
   - Real-time loss, perplexity, and learning rate graphs
   - One-click smoke testing for all 5 model families
   - Model configurator (rank, alpha, batch size, LR)
   - Integrated CTest runner with pass/fail reporting

4. MediDocs AI Clinical Application
   - Hospital policy document ingestion (PDF + plain text)
   - Hybrid TF-IDF + BM25 retrieval engine
   - Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) at retrieval layer
   - Version conflict detection (active vs superseded)
   - Responsible abstention for out-of-domain queries
   - Comprehensive security audit trail
   - 50-test automated evaluation benchmark

5. Evaluation and Benchmarking
   - WikiText-2 language model perplexity evaluation
   - MMLU multi-task accuracy evaluation
   - QNLI natural language inference evaluation
   - RAG quality metrics (Recall@5, Citation Correctness,
     Answer Correctness, Abstention F1, RBAC Violations)

OUT OF SCOPE:

1. GPU / NPU hardware acceleration (CPU-only in this version)
2. INT4 / INT8 quantized training (FP32 only currently)
3. Models larger than 1B parameters without quantization
4. Real-time speech-to-text or voice input
5. Integration with live EHR / FHIR systems
6. Federated multi-node training coordination
7. Production deployment with persistent database storage
8. Mobile (Android/iOS) native deployment in this version




================================================================
                        1.3 MOTIVATION
================================================================

The motivation for EdgeTune emerges from three converging
real-world pressures:

MOTIVATION 1 — THE PRIVACY CRISIS IN AI ADOPTION

The widespread adoption of LLMs in enterprise and healthcare
settings has been severely hampered by data privacy concerns.
Organizations that stand to benefit most from AI — hospitals
managing patient records, law firms holding privileged
communications, financial institutions processing transaction
data — are precisely the ones that cannot send their data to
cloud-based fine-tuning APIs.

HIPAA in the United States, GDPR in Europe, and the DPDP Act
in India all impose strict requirements on how personal and
sensitive data may be processed. Cloud fine-tuning APIs
inherently violate these requirements because they require
transmitting sensitive training data to third-party servers.

The practical consequence is a two-tier AI landscape: large
technology companies with private GPU infrastructure can
deploy custom-adapted models; everyone else must use generic
pretrained models that hallucinate domain-specific facts or
accept the legal and ethical risks of cloud data processing.

EdgeTune eliminates this disparity by making fine-tuning
entirely local — ensuring that the power to adapt an AI
model is available to any organization, regardless of whether
they have cloud access, GPU hardware, or Python expertise.

MOTIVATION 2 — THE HALLUCINATION PROBLEM IN CLINICAL AI

LLMs are increasingly being explored for clinical
documentation, diagnostic support, and policy lookup in
healthcare settings. The critical barrier is hallucination —
the tendency of LLMs to generate confidently-worded but
factually incorrect responses when they lack specific domain
knowledge.

A general-purpose LLM asked about a specific hospital's
medication cold-chain excursion reporting procedure might
generate a plausible-sounding response that contradicts the
actual SOP. In clinical practice, such a response could lead
to protocol violations, patient harm, or regulatory sanctions.

MediDocs AI addresses this directly through three mechanisms:
  1. RAG grounds every answer in verified document passages
  2. Abstention prevents answers when confidence is low
  3. LoRA fine-tuning on clinical instruction data ensures
     that the model's output format matches institutional
     documentation standards exactly

MOTIVATION 3 — THE RESEARCH GAP IN ON-EDGE TRAINING

A thorough survey of existing on-device AI frameworks reveals
a critical gap: every major framework (TFLite, ONNX Runtime,
llama.cpp, MLC-LLM) supports inference only. None implements
backward passes, gradient computation, or parameter updates.
Fine-tuning has been treated as a server-side operation since
the inception of deep learning.

Yet the mathematical operations required for LoRA fine-tuning
— low-rank matrix products, chain-rule gradient propagation,
adaptive moment updates — are well-understood and modest in
computational complexity. The barrier is not mathematical
difficulty but the absence of a production-quality native
implementation.

EdgeTune fills this gap. It proves that the complete PEFT
training pipeline can be implemented in standard C++17,
runs correctly on commodity hardware, and produces
HuggingFace-compatible adapter outputs — without a single
line of Python in the training path.




================================================================
                        1.4 OBJECTIVE
================================================================

The EdgeTune project pursues the following primary and
secondary objectives:

PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:

Objective 1 — Zero-Dependency Native Fine-Tuning Engine
  Build a complete LLM fine-tuning engine in pure C++17
  with zero Python runtime dependency in the training
  execution path. Every mathematical operation — forward
  pass, loss computation, automatic differentiation, and
  parameter update — must execute natively without any
  external ML framework.

Objective 2 — Faithful LoRA Implementation
  Implement Parameter Efficient Fine-Tuning via Low-Rank
  Adaptation (LoRA) natively in C++17. Adapter outputs
  must be numerically correct and saved in the standard
  HuggingFace PEFT SafeTensors format, enabling
  interoperability with the Python ecosystem.

Objective 3 — Multi-Architecture Model Support
  Support a minimum of three distinct LLM architectural
  families under a single unified Auto API, covering
  variations in positional encoding (absolute vs RoPE),
  attention mechanism (MHA vs GQA), normalization
  (LayerNorm vs RMSNorm), and feed-forward activation
  (GELU vs GeGLU vs SwiGLU).

Objective 4 — Privacy-by-Design Architecture
  Guarantee that all training data, model weights, and
  adapter parameters remain local to the executing machine
  at all times. The system must make zero external network
  calls during any phase of the training pipeline.

Objective 5 — Clinical RAG Application with RBAC
  Build a production-quality clinical document intelligence
  application (MediDocs AI) that demonstrates EdgeTune's
  real-world value by combining LoRA fine-tuning with a
  hybrid retrieval pipeline, enforcing access control at
  the retrieval kernel, and achieving measurable
  hallucination reduction.

SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:

Objective 6 — Measurable Hallucination Reduction
  Demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in
  hallucination rate through the combination of RAG and
  LoRA fine-tuning, targeting a final rate below 3.0%
  compared to the 42.0% baseline.

Objective 7 — Comprehensive Evaluation Framework
  Establish a standardized 50-test benchmark suite for
  RAG quality evaluation covering six categories: direct
  policy lookup, multi-passage synthesis, version conflict
  detection, source conflict resolution, abstention
  correctness, and RBAC security enforcement.

Objective 8 — Web-Based Training Dashboard
  Provide a lightweight, Python-stdlib-only web dashboard
  for real-time training monitoring — live loss curves,
  console log streaming, model configurator, and test
  runner — requiring no cloud connectivity or heavy
  frontend framework.

Objective 9 — Academic Contribution
  Produce a research paper documenting the methodology,
  implementation, and evaluation results, submitted to
  a peer-reviewed venue in the embedded systems and
  systems for ML space.

                       1.5 APPLICATION
================================================================

EdgeTune and MediDocs AI have direct, production-relevant
applications across multiple industries and use cases:

APPLICATION 1 — CLINICAL DOCUMENT INTELLIGENCE (HEALTHCARE)
  The primary demonstration application. Hospital staff
  query internal SOPs, pharmacy protocols, clinical forms,
  and administrative policies through natural language.
  The system returns grounded, cited answers with role-
  based access control enforced at the retrieval level.

  Real-world value:
  - Nurses instantly access medication storage protocols
    without searching through paper binders
  - Attending physicians query sepsis bundle criteria
    during critical care situations
  - Hospital administrators audit policy compliance
  - Clinical interns guided to general-access resources
    while restricted protocols remain protected

  Compliance alignment: HIPAA-compatible by design —
  patient data never leaves the hospital's local system.

APPLICATION 2 — LEGAL DOCUMENT INTELLIGENCE
  Law firms and legal departments can fine-tune EdgeTune
  on internal case law, contract templates, and regulatory
  filings. RAG retrieval grounds answers in verified legal
  sources. LoRA adaptation ensures outputs conform to
  the firm's precise document formatting standards.

  Key benefit: Attorney-client privilege is preserved —
  no confidential legal documents are ever transmitted
  to external cloud services.

APPLICATION 3 — ENTERPRISE KNOWLEDGE MANAGEMENT
  Large organizations with extensive internal documentation
  (engineering wikis, HR policies, product manuals, safety
  procedures) can deploy MediDocs AI as an internal
  knowledge assistant. Employees ask natural language
  questions and receive answers grounded in company-
  approved documentation — not generic internet knowledge.

APPLICATION 4 — REGULATED FINANCIAL SERVICES
  Financial institutions subject to SOX, PCI-DSS, or
  Basel III compliance requirements can fine-tune EdgeTune
  on proprietary financial models, risk frameworks, and
  compliance procedures. All processing remains on-premise,
  satisfying data residency requirements.

APPLICATION 5 — GOVERNMENT AND DEFENSE
  Air-gapped environments where external network
  connectivity is prohibited can use EdgeTune for
  document intelligence and model adaptation. No cloud
  dependency means EdgeTune is deployable on classified
  networks, military systems, and critical infrastructure.

APPLICATION 6 — EDUCATION AND E-LEARNING
  Educational institutions can fine-tune language models
  on course-specific content, textbooks, and assessments.
  Students interact with a domain-adapted AI tutor that
  cites specific chapters and pages — without sending
  proprietary educational content to third-party APIs.

APPLICATION 7 — RESEARCH AND ACADEMIC USE
  Researchers in NLP, systems, and embedded AI can use
  EdgeTune as a reproducible baseline for:
  - On-device training experiments
  - PEFT algorithm development and comparison
  - RAG pipeline design and evaluation
  - Privacy-preserving federated learning research

APPLICATION 8 — WEB-BASED TRAINING DASHBOARD
  Data scientists and ML engineers who prefer web-based
  workflows can use the EdgeTune dashboard to:
  - Monitor training runs in real time
  - Trigger smoke tests on all supported model families
  - Configure and compare LoRA hyperparameters
  - Inspect granular test results from the CTest suite
  — all from a browser, with zero cloud account required.
